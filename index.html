WELCOME TO THE TABLE

It’s all in your hands.

Small touches, cushioned catches, fast drives. Every shot still comes from foot contact. Each match serve starts with a one-second ready period: position your handles before the ball goes live.

01 / Original handles

Drag any handle to slide and rotate its rod. In landscape: up/down slides, left rotates forward. In portrait: left/right slides, up rotates forward. Small movements give small touches. Fast physical foot movement can hit hard. Ordinary rotation is a direct distance-to-angle control, not a charged-shot trigger. A deliberate flick from a held pin is the assisted release gesture. Slide while rotating for angled contacts. The ball keeps rolling outside real contact. A relaxed grip yields on impact; small slow contact near the top can hold it.

02 / Front & back pins

Select a rod, then tap its PIN button. Slide a figure over a slow nearby ball. The game chooses front or back from the ball’s actual side of the rod; FRONT PIN or BACK PIN appears only when the foot has made a valid top contact. A very close ball is gently rolled into position by the foot. There is no distant attraction or large stopping zone. Small cross-axis wobble will not cancel an armed pin. Slide gently to walk; lift your finger and re-grip without losing a supported ball. A hard sideways pull can slip. Tap PIN again to lift clear without shooting. A forward flick releases a front pin into a rollover, or a back pin into a forward drive. For a manual open-hand front-pin shot, lift, move beside the ball, wind behind it, and strike.

The 3D button cycles through your stable side view, a 2D contact cross-section, and Off. Both teams’ nearest figures are shown. A translucent figure in 2D is outside the ball’s cross-section. The ball outline stays visible under a foot. Its soccer panels use the same camera projection and physical radius as the table. Rotation comes from the simulated orientation; genuine skids and backspin remain. Fast rotation receives a small motion blur instead of artificially slowing down. Use Table only to hide cameras and bottom controls. The on-table handles still work; hold one still briefly to arm a nearby slow-ball pin. The small corner buttons restore the interface or open the menu. Camera and bottom controls can also be hidden independently. Slow controlled raises keep their angle; auto-ready returns only after a fast flick.

03 / Two players

Switch to 2 players for shared-screen multiplayer. Mint uses the lower handles; Coral uses the upper handles. Coral’s view and dock face the other player. In landscape Mint swipes left to shoot and Coral swipes right; in portrait Mint swipes up and Coral swipes down. Each player has a PIN button and can hold two rods simultaneously. There is no online multiplayer. A resting ball is not automatically dead: move or rotate any reachable rod. The Stuck ball notice appears only when the stopped ball is outside every figure’s full mechanical reach. Auto re-serve waits three seconds, then gives both sides the usual one-second ready period. Turning Auto re-serve off keeps the notice and manual button. Reachable or pinned balls never trigger it. Practice reset is a separate action.

04 / Keyboard + mouse

Both inputs are always available, with no mode switch. Mint’s rods run left to right in landscape (bottom to top in portrait): Q/A + Z keeper, W/S + X defense, E/D + C midfield, R/F + V attack. First pair moves up/down; last key shoots. Hold Shift or Space for faster sliding, or Alt + shoot for a soft pass. 1–4 selects, G pins, B raises/lowers, left/right arrows gently rotate the selected rod. Keyboard fine speed, fast speed and power are adjustable separately from mouse/touch slide and turn sensitivity.

In two-player, 7–0 selects Coral’s rod, arrows slide/rotate it, Enter shoots, / pins, . rolls over. P pauses. A pointer holding a rod has priority over that rod’s keys. Slow manual positioning keeps its angle after release. Only a coherent fast whip followed by release requests auto-ready; a long slow raise or a tiny twitch does not.

The physics, honestly.

A spinning 3D ball, compound solid figures, finite rod inertia, contact friction, gravity, and physical goal openings. A 240 Hz clock uses extra collision substeps for fast motion. Human and bot inputs drive the same rod motors.

This is a playable simulation prototype—not a measured digital twin or a full ITSF rules engine. The optional pin grip uses a bounded, breakable contact support and lateral tracking assist after real foot contact. It is an accessibility approximation, not strict no-slip rolling. Real table materials, wear, flex, and every professional technique are not fully reproduced. The five named bot styles each support five difficulty levels. Their profile labels are fictional, not calibrated Elo ratings. Elite adds contact rehearsal, moving-defender re-aiming and shot-outcome learning that persists across rallies; it is not a verified professional ranking.

Technique references: British Foosball Association’s pin, snake, brush passing, push/pull and push/pull-kick guides. The library covers 13 move families, not every trick ever played. A practice demonstrator uses the same rod inputs; grab a handle to take over. Full engineering notes and tests are in the source package.
